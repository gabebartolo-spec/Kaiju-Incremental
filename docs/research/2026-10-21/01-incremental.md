# What makes active incrementals loop well (research for Kaiju Incremental)

Researched 2026-10-21. Scope: active incrementals (Nodebuster, Astro Prospector, Chef Knight, Bills Must Be Paid) and run-based neighbours (Brotato, Vampire Survivors, Halls of Torment, Ball x Pit). Cookie Clicker / AdVenture Capitalist maths used only as contrast. Aggregator figures (vaporlens.app, Raijin) are automated syntheses or estimates and are labelled as such.

## Key findings

1. **The benchmark total length is 3-5 hours, and "too short" is the top complaint even when length is praised.** Nodebuster (97% positive of 10,675 Steam reviews, released 13 Aug 2024, about US$3) takes about 3-6 h by player reports; one reviewer finished in "just over four hours" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3107330/Nodebuster/); [vaporlens summary](https://vaporlens.app/app/3107330/nodebuster.md); [Mancunion review](https://mancunion.com/2025/05/08/nodebuster-review-a-short-and-sweet-incremental-game/)). Astro Prospector's developer advertised "around 5 hours ... +200 upgrades" ([itch devlog](https://delunado.itch.io/astro-prospector/devlog/986158/astro-prospector-is-out-now-)). Chef Knight runs about 3-3.5 h; "too short" is its most-cited criticism (weight 0.97), while "short but balanced length" is also praised (0.62) ([vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/4304930/chef_knight.md)). The lesson: players accept 3-5 h for a cheap game and remember it fondly if it ends before boredom, but they always ask for more endgame. A 3-5 h target fits the genre.

2. **Early runs are very short, and an upgrade in every run is the pacing floor.** Astro Prospector's first runs "can end in under a minute" and grow into full bullet-hell runs as durability and timer upgrades stack ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/astro-prospector-20250715-349323/)). The Nodebuster sub-genre is defined as smashing objects against a time limit, with upgrades raising damage, time and money ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/08/20/part-2-the-week-of-the-golden-age/)). The most common pacing complaint on Nodebuster-likes is a slow start. One player asked for early prices to be lowered "so that the player can afford a new upgrade every run" ([itch comment on Shroomstead](https://itch.io/post/14762029)). Brotato builds the same ramp into one run: wave 1 lasts 20 s, each wave adds 5 s up to 60 s, there is a shop after every wave, and it ends at wave 20 ([Brotato wiki](https://brotato.wiki.spellsandguns.com/Waves)).

3. **Costs should rise geometrically while power rises with multipliers. Gentle growth rates come from idle games; any second-scale timing targets are a hobbyist's guesses.** Kongregate's maths uses cost = base x rate^owned, with AdVenture Capitalist's rate at 1.07. Production grows linearly, with stacking x2 multipliers at 25 and 50 owned. Exponential cost always overtakes polynomial production, and that crossing is the "wall" which prompts a prestige ([Game Developer, Pecorella part I](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-i)). One indie developer suggests a default rate of 1.15, a first purchase within 60 s, a meaningful choice within 5 min, and a next run that feels visibly faster within 30 s ([dev.to](https://dev.to/aguier/i-built-7-idle-games-in-30-days-what-i-learned-about-incremental-design-5d3f)). That post is AI-assisted and untested, so treat these as starting values, not evidence. I found no Nodebuster or Astro Prospector cost tables.

4. **The top complaint about Nodebuster is the late game going trivial and AFK.** Its biggest criticism theme is unbalanced progression (weight 0.58): slow early, then the player is overpowered, bosses stop mattering and the late game becomes an AFK grind ([vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/3107330/nodebuster.md)). The Mancunion reviewer noticed the same drift from active input to "idle-friendly" play near the end ([Mancunion](https://mancunion.com/2025/05/08/nodebuster-review-a-short-and-sweet-incremental-game/)). Vampire Survivors draws the same complaint: once a build survives 30 minutes standing still, runs become predictable ([Boss Rush](https://bossrush.net/2022/12/game-review-vampire-survivors/?nb=1)). For a zero-AFK game this is the main trap: power must outrun the city, but the city has to keep pushing back.

5. **Stat-only trees are a named weakness; new verbs and new tabs carry the reveal.** Nodebuster reveals content as new screens and resources rather than bigger numbers. It starts with the tree and the breach; then blue enemies drop nodes, a milestones tab opens, then a crypto-mine tab, a prestige level that raises enemy stats and rewards, and a final lab ([Mancunion](https://mancunion.com/2025/05/08/nodebuster-review-a-short-and-sweet-incremental-game/)). Players still note that most tree nodes are plain stat boosts and some cost too much ([search summary of itch feedback](https://itch.io/post/13700295)). Chef Knight's praised tree "becomes more engaging as players advance", and each zone has its own gimmick and themed weapon unlocks ([vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/4304930/chef_knight.md)).

6. **Physical destruction with strong 3D game feel is the proven way to stand out in this sub-genre right now.** Bills Must Be Paid is a Nodebuster-like about smashing piggy banks with a hammer: 2 devs, about 7 months, US$6.99. It made about US$163,842 on day one and about 330,000 units in 20 days; its demo median play time was 36 min. The analysis credits its 3D graphics and game feel over pixel-art rivals, plus a recurring bill that forces a prestige and adds tension ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/08/20/part-2-the-week-of-the-golden-age/)). Raijin estimates about 180k copies and 97% positive of 7,627 reviews ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/4421010/Bills_Must_Be_Paid)). This is the closest commercial proof for "smash things in 3D, number goes up".

7. **Escalation needs both a per-run crescendo and a fixed run cap.** Vampire Survivors runs to 30 min, building from one small attack to clearing hundreds of enemies at once; every run that ends early reads as a near-success ([Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/?p=3248930); [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/vampire-survivors-how-developers-used-gambling-psychology-to-create-a-bafta-winning-game-203613)). Halls of Torment sells "quick and casual 30 minute runs" with quest-based meta-progression ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2218750/Halls_of_Torment/)). Ball x Pit stages rarely run past 15 min and losing never feels punishing, because the base grows between runs ([Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/72918/ball-x-pit-switch-review)). Run caps range from about 1 min (early incrementals) to 15-30 min (survivors-likes).

8. **Meta-progression that gates the next run's content beats pure stat carry-over, but it should not tax the run.** Ball x Pit has 70+ buildings that grow between runs; reviewers spend 20+ h in 15-60 min sessions. Its main criticism is that construction must be finished by bouncing balls off a building during the next run, which taxes the run ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/ball-x-pit-review/); [Trending Now](https://trendingnow.games/reviews/ball-x-pit-review)). In Nodebuster, prestige raises enemy stats and rewards and unlocks a new boss, which is meta-progression that also makes the next run harder and bigger ([Mancunion](https://mancunion.com/2025/05/08/nodebuster-review-a-short-and-sweet-incremental-game/)).

9. **The ending should be a clear final act, and in the reviewed games it is often the weakest part.** Nodebuster ends when you deploy a "godvirus" in the lab for 1,000 netcoin; some players find the ending fitting, others abrupt ([Mancunion](https://mancunion.com/2025/05/08/nodebuster-review-a-short-and-sweet-incremental-game/); [vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/3107330/nodebuster.md)). Chef Knight's final level is its second-largest complaint (0.54): visual chaos and an unreadable character ([vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/4304930/chef_knight.md)). Screen-filling effects are the power fantasy, but past a point they become a readability defect. Nodebuster also draws complaints about screen shake and flashing causing eye strain ([vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/3107330/nodebuster.md)).

## For Kaiju Incremental (ranked, simplest viable first)

1. **Fix the pacing floor in data: an upgrade affordable after every rampage, and a first purchase inside about 60 s.** Use geometric costs (cost = base x rate^level, rate between about 1.07 and 1.15 per node line) in `data/`, plus a headless sim test that plays seeded rampages and asserts at least one affordable node per run through the first hour. Cost: about 1-2 days (data table plus one test); low risk. This serves findings 2 and 3 and guards against Nodebuster's slow-start complaint.
2. **Cap and grow the rampage timer: about 45-90 s at first, rising through upgrades to a 10-15 min ceiling late game.** Timer and size are the cheapest "bigger than the last" levers (Astro Prospector, Brotato). Cost: about 1 day if a run timer already exists; low risk; needs one playtest to set the start value.
3. **Make the city scale with the kaiju so the late game never goes AFK.** As the kaiju grows, spawn taller blocks, denser districts and more tanks and helicopters, and add the Nodebuster-style prestige rule of "harder enemies, bigger rewards". This directly answers the genre's top complaint (finding 4) and the director's zero-AFK rule. Cost: about 3-5 days (difficulty table keyed to stage, plus a sim fixture proving the city's resistance rises with stage); medium risk (balance).
4. **Spend tree reveals on new verbs and stage body plans, not stats.** Rule of thumb: about 1 node in 4 should change what the player does or sees (new attack, new prey, new district, the next growth stage); the rest are stats. Reveal only the next ring of nodes, and grow the tree outward like Nodebuster's tab-by-tab reveal. Cost: design time plus about 1-2 weeks per new verb; medium risk (scope). Challenge: keep the verb count small (for example 6-8 across 3-5 h) rather than many shallow ones.
5. **Build one authored finale: a final stage and a final city, with a readable climax.** Chef Knight's chaotic finale and Nodebuster's abrupt one are the warnings. Budget a readability pass (caps on particles and screen shake, a kaiju outline) for the biggest rampages. Cost: about 1-2 weeks; medium risk. A screen-shake toggle costs under a day and answers Nodebuster's eye-strain complaints.
6. **Optional, later: meta-progression that unlocks districts or kaiju species, never chores inside a run.** Avoid Ball x Pit's construction tax. Cost: weeks; higher scope risk. Do it only if the tree alone does not carry 3-5 h.

Total-length target: 3-5 h to the ending, which matches Nodebuster, Astro Prospector and Chef Knight. Expect "too short" in reviews regardless, and keep a post-ending endless or higher-difficulty mode as a cheap later add rather than padding the main path.

**Evidence gaps:** No developer postmortem or GDC talk was found for Nodebuster, Astro Prospector or Chef Knight, and no published cost tables, upgrade counts (beyond Astro Prospector's "+200") or per-run timer values. The time-to-upgrade targets come from one anecdotal, AI-assisted blog post. Review themes come largely from vaporlens automated syntheses and Raijin estimates, not raw review samples. Halls of Torment's meta-progression grind is unquantified, and the Brotato wiki contradicts itself on wave 20's length. The 1.15 rate is not in the Kongregate source.
