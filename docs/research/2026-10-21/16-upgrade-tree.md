# Upgrade tree look, pathing and cadence (research for Kaiju Incremental)

Researched 2026-10-21. This builds on `01-incremental.md` (cited as "01"), which already covers total length, the "an upgrade every run" floor, geometric costs, the late-game AFK trap and "about 1 node in 4 should be a verb". It does not repeat those points. Repo state read (read-only): `data/upgrades.json`, `sim/upgrade_tree.gd`, `scenes/upgrade_screen.gd`, `scenes/tree_area.gd`, `scenes/upgrade_node.gd`, `tools/pacing.gd` (header), `docs/ROADMAP.md` phase E, build-2 notes b2-2/15/16/17/24/27/28, playtest note p1-8, and decisions D-039, D-040, D-077 and D-098.

**What the tree is today (my count from the data):** 31 definitions, of which 19 are shared and 4 belong to each species. That makes 23 nodes and 87 levels per species. The layout is a fixed 8 x 6 grid (`pos` in data) that fills the screen. It is Control-based, with no pan or zoom, and draws straight 4 px `draw_line` links that are lit (GOOD) or unlit (LOCKED). Every node is visible from the start: locked nodes have a dashed outline. A purchase triggers only `Sfx.ui("ui_confirm")`. The reptilian tree by size tier (tier = evolutions in a node's prerequisite chain, as in `tier_of`):

| Tier | Nodes | Levels | Total cost (biomass) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (young) | 11 | 45 | 30,477 |
| 2 (Behemoth) | 6 | 23 | 210,058 |
| 3 (Colossus) | 5 | 16 | 1,615,440 |
| 4 (after Apex) | 1 | 3 | 845,820 |

The tree thins as the kaiju grows: later stages offer fewer purchases while runs get longer. That is the inverse of "every rampage bigger than the last".

## Key findings

1. **A tree can look like a sprawling map while being mostly a straight line, and players do not mind.** Final Fantasy X's sphere grid has 828 nodes. Chad Birch's analysis found it "extremely linear", with stretches of over 50 nodes without a branch and only 2-3 warp spheres giving real choice. It still feels rich, because players spend a few points at a time and the layout hides how straight the path is ([Kotaku on GameInternals](https://kotaku.com/final-fantasy-x-was-less-complex-than-it-seemed-5766535)). In a buy-everything incremental, branches decide the *order* you take things in, not exclusive builds. The layout's job is to look like territory being conquered and to set pace, not to support theorycrafting.

2. **Nodebuster's tree is about 97 nodes, levels run 1-15, and the nodes themselves gate new verbs and screens.** I counted 97 nodes from a community guide, in four groups: Main Attack 22, Secondary Attacks 15, Survivability 34 and Miscellaneous 26. Three "Tabs" nodes open the milestones, crypto-mine and lab screens, and 9 "???" story nodes stay hidden until reached ([Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3414691532); the guide is marked as removed from the community, so treat it as a snapshot). The same guide credits the unfolding tree with "the feeling of progression", while players still flag most nodes as plain stat boosts (01, finding 5). The guide gives no costs and does not describe the radial shape. Astro Prospector advertises "+200 upgrades" (01) and its later Endless mode 420+ ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/sub/1237067)). Genre trees therefore run at roughly 100-400 nodes over 3-5 h, against our 23 per species. b2-28 ("double or even triple") is within the genre band, not over it.

3. **Node size and shape say how much a node matters.** Path of Exile's tree has 1,325 passives ([official tree](https://pathofexile.com/passive-skill-tree)) in three visual tiers. Small passives lead toward a notable and give smaller versions of its bonus. Notables are mid-sized. Keystones are the largest and rarest, and they change the build ([Vulkk guide](https://vulkk.com/2025/02/08/path-of-exile-2-passive-tree-guide/); [Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/poe-2/guides/passive-skill-tree)). Clusters of small nodes leading to one notable are the "path toward a reward" read. Our nodes are all the same rectangle. Only colour separates evolutions (red) and ultimates (hazard tape), and colour alone is weak at 10 feet.

4. **Partial reveal makes players read the tree as a map to explore, and showing the goal lets them plan.** A procedural skill-tree project grades visibility by graph distance. Nodes next to owned ones are buyable, farther ones show only their type, and the farthest show only the connection structure ([pijul procgen-skill-tree](https://nest.pijul.com/ambersz/procgen-skill-tree/changes/QGNWR4HBY4JPZMA6LE4M7EY3UCYSYVX4TQAUB3QHODSSGKEDO5SQC)). Nodebuster hides its story nodes as "???" (finding 2). Slay the Spire is the contrast case. It reveals one act's map at a time, but in full, so players plan routes by looking ahead along strict branching tracks ([ludo.guide](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/slay-the-spire/pathing-risk-assessment/map-generation-and-branching); [wiki.gg, under construction](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Map_Generation)). Combined: show the next step in full, the next stage's region as shapes, and the next evolution as a visible but distant landmark.

5. **Players hate a tree view that moves on its own or forgets their zoom.** On a Nodebuster-like, a player asked that "the zoom-out level shouldn't be reset" on returning to the tree, and the developer agreed to fix it ([itch, Slay Them All](https://itch.io/post/15730836)). On another, auto-centring on the last purchase was called "all over the place haphazardly, which is confusing". The developer had added it because players lost the tree after accidental pans ([itch, Chop Chains](https://itch.io/post/15563206)). The rule: keep the player's view, give a "home" key, and move the camera only for a scripted reveal such as a new stage's region.

6. **Purchase bursts at milestones, and bumpy rather than smooth progress, are the cadence to aim for.** Pecorella's idle maths shows milestone multipliers causing bursts of rapid purchases, says progress should feel "bumpy" with slow and fast stretches, and notes that the novelty of big numbers "is largely gone", so the fun must come from new features ([Game Developer, part III](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-iii)). For us the milestones are the evolutions. Make the run after an evolution a deliberate burst of cheap new nodes (a fast stretch) and the run-up to the next one a visible climb (a slow stretch). The two failure modes are the spikes in p1-8: one upgrade, then eight, then one, with no authored reason. D-098 (option A, "steady upgrades", 2-4 buys per death) already sets the floor and ceiling.

7. **Juice means maximum output for minimum input: a purchase should be an event, not a colour change.** Jonasson and Purho's "Juice it or lose it" (GDC Europe 2012) builds feel from tweening, squash and stretch, screen shake, particles and small sounds ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-is-your-game-juicy-enough-); [RPG Playground summary](https://rpgplayground.com/research-making-a-juicy-game/)). Folmer Kelly's counter-talk warns that overdone polish costs immersion ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-indies-resist-the-urge-to-juice-it-or-lose-it-)). Specific tree juice (light travelling along a line to newly opened nodes, node pops, rising pitch per level) is common in the genre, but I found no primary source describing it. It is design inference.

8. **Felt power comes from a meaningfully stronger next run, not from the number going up.** Kasavin says the Hades Mirror is meant to make the player "meaningfully more powerful from one playthrough to the next", combining the player's knowledge with the character's strength, and that Supergiant avoided addiction tactics ([Tom's Guide interview](https://www.tomsguide.com/uk/features/hades-exclusive-interview-supergiant)). Critics note the Mirror simply makes the game easier ([Quarter to Three](https://forum.quartertothree.com/t/hades-supergiant-games/139167?page=66)). That is the same AFK and trivial-late-game risk as 01 finding 4. Power has to show on screen in the next rampage, and the city has to push back (D-039: the city grows with each evolution).

9. **Godot 4.7 has every piece needed. The constraint is text at 4K, not draw cost.** `_draw()` runs once, its commands are cached until `queue_redraw()`, and many unconnected lines in one `draw_multiline` call are faster than separate calls ([custom drawing, 4.7](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/2d/custom_drawing_in_2d.html)). Line2D has `texture_mode` TILE (needs `texture_repeat`), a gradient and `width_curve`. Anti-aliased lines are not batched ([Line2D, 4.7](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_line2d.html)). A canvas_item shader gets `UV`, `TIME`, `COLOR` and `TEXTURE` in `fragment()`, enough for a scrolling "flow" on lit links ([canvas_item shaders, 4.7](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/canvas_item_shader.html)). Focus: automatic neighbour guessing "can behave unexpectedly" in complex layouts, so set explicit `focus_neighbor_*`, and `grab_focus.call_deferred()` at start ([GUI navigation, 4.7](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/ui/gui_navigation.html)). 100-200 Button nodes plus one `_draw` for all links is well within budget; that is my estimate, not a benchmark. The real constraint is that zooming a Control scales its text, which the current `TreeArea` explicitly avoids ("never scaled, so text keeps its size on the TV").

## Sample first-hour cadence (my estimate, a design target for the pacing bot, not a measurement)

The assumptions come from repo data and D-098, rounded. Base run 40 s (`tuning.json`); Endless Rage adds 6 s a level; about 20 s in the tree per visit (`tools/pacing.gd` TREE_SECONDS); deaths end some early runs; the bot reaches evolve1 at 0.24-0.27 h (D-098). That gives about 1-1.3 min per loop at the young stage and about 1.5-2 min after the first evolution.

| Minute | Run # | Purchases that visit | Big moment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | 1 | 1 (first claw level) | First buy inside 60 s (01) |
| 2-3 | 2-3 | 1-2 | Health or claws level 2; the run visibly lasts longer |
| 4-5 | 4 | 1 + **verb** | **Tail Sweep**: first new verb inside 5 min |
| 6-10 | 5-8 | 2-3 each | Rage levels, the run reaches about 60 s; Hoarder: bigger biomass numbers |
| 10-12 | 9-10 | 2 + **verb** | **Momentum**: second verb; the evolution landmark becomes affordable soon |
| 13-14 | 11-12 | 1-2 (saving) | The slow stretch: the player saves for the evolution |
| ~15-16 | 13 | **EVOLVE: Behemoth** | **Stage up**: new body, bigger city (D-039), new ring revealed |
| 16-20 | 14-16 | 3-4 each (burst) | Cheap tier-2 ring opens: the fast stretch |
| 20-30 | 17-22 | 2-3 each | A verb or visible node about every 3-5 runs (Signature Power, Shearing Tail) |
| 30-40 | 23-28 | 2-3 each | Midpoint notable: new prey or a new army type that the city sends |
| 40-55 | 29-36 | 1-3 each | The climb toward the Colossus landmark |
| ~55-70 | ~37-42 | **EVOLVE: Colossus** | Stage two, then the next burst |

Run-rate check (my estimate): 3-5 h at about 1.5-2.5 min per loop is about 100-170 runs. At D-098's 2-4 buys per visit, about 1.5-2.5 levels a run on average, that comes to about 150-400 levels. Today's 87 levels per species run out early, which matches D-039's finding that the tree clears in 0.9 h on one city. A 2-3x node count (b2-28) at similar levels per node gives about 175-260 levels, which lands inside the band. b2-28 and p1-8 are therefore one sum, not two separate tasks.

## For Kaiju Incremental (ranked, simplest viable first)

These are within the director's rulings: copy rule b2-15/16/17/24/27, D-077 stats panel, D-040 ultimate button, D-098 steady upgrades, D-039 city grows per stage, phase E before F, balance settled by his playtest.

1. **Write the 2-3x tree as rings per stage, with the counts growing outward, and make the pacing bot enforce the cadence.** Data only, plus `tools/pacing.gd`. Target about 50-65 nodes per species. Add a `kind` field to each node (`stat`, `verb`, `stage`) and make each later ring *larger* than the one before, for example about 14 / 16 / 18 / 10 nodes, instead of today's 11 / 6 / 5 / 1. About 1 node in 4 should be a verb or visible change (01). Give each stage a cheap "entry burst" of 3-4 nodes just past its evolution and a costlier climb before the next one (finding 6). Then add asserts to the bot's seeded runs: 1-4 buys per visit (never 0, never the p1-8 "8"); no more than 5 runs between `verb` or `stage` nodes; evolutions within ±20% of target times (e1 about 0.25 h, e2 about 1 h, e3 about 2.3 h, end 3-5 h; my targets). Cost: 1-2 days of data and copy, plus about half a day of bot asserts. Conflict to flag: the verb nodes need D-phase verbs (b2-2 tail as an autonomous limb) and E's copy pass. Write placeholder verbs only from powers that already exist.

2. **Make buying an event: presentation only, inside the current screen.** On a buy, the node pops (a 0.1-0.15 s scale tween), the link to each newly unlocked child lights from parent to child (a tweened 0-1 value read in `TreeArea._draw`, about 0.3 s), the children fade in, and the confirm sound rises a step per level. An evolution buy gets a bigger beat: a short flash and screen punch, then the existing `scenes/evolution_reveal.gd`. Respect 01's eye-strain note: small shake, and a toggle. Cost: about 1 day; no sim change.

3. **Show the next step, and the next stage only as shapes.** Add a `hidden` state to `UpgradeNode.show_state`: nodes two or more links beyond anything owned draw as dim silhouettes, with their shape and kind icon but no name or price. The next evolution always shows as a landmark at full size. Drop the "BRANCH" band and caption and the role line (b2-24, b2-27). Cost: about half a day, one extra state and one graph-distance pass.

4. **Shape language by kind, readable at 10 feet.** Three sizes, as in Path of Exile: small round stat nodes, larger shaped verb nodes with an icon, and the largest evolution keystones. Kind must read from shape and size, never colour alone. Keep the 24 px floor; the current FONT_MIN of 18 px is justified in code against a 28 px cap-height bar at 4K, so re-measure it in F. Cost: about 1 day for the placeholder style in E; the bespoke art goes into F's b2-14.

5. **Replace the fixed 8 x 6 grid with an outward map: polar layout from data, pan without zoom.** 50-65 nodes do not fit 48 grid cells at the current `MIN_SIZE` (120 x 100 px per cell), so this is forced by b2-28, not optional. Put the young ring at the centre and later stages further out, so the radial shape grows bigger rings naturally. Replace `pos` with `ring` and `angle` (or keep `pos` as a hand-placed override). Pan by offsetting a world Control (or use a SubViewport and Camera2D). Keep node size fixed so text never scales (finding 9). Remember the view between visits, give a home key, and move the camera automatically only for an evolution reveal (finding 5). Compute focus neighbours geometrically (nearest node in each d-pad direction) at layout time. Cost: about 3-5 days, including focus, a GUI-input test and a screen-critic capture. **Flag:** this is a layout decision with a visible look, so it needs the director's pick from labelled captures (radial against a left-to-right "map"). The roadmap puts the UI redesign in F "on the final camera and tree". Recommend making the layout choice in E and the skin in F.

6. **Last: a flowing shader on owned links.** Line2D in TILE mode, or a quad per link, with a canvas_item shader that scrolls a soft pulse along UV.x using `TIME`. Owned paths read as "alive" and the newest path pulses brightest. Check it in both Forward+ and gl_compatibility. Cost: about half a day; purely cosmetic, so only after items 1-5.

Why the order: item 1 alone fixes the measurable problems (too few nodes, the p1-8 spikes, a tree that thins out as you grow). Items 2-4 are cheap, presentation-only and work in today's grid. Item 5 is the only expensive one, and b2-28 forces it.

**Evidence gaps:** I found no developer postmortem or GDC talk on the tree screens of Nodebuster, Astro Prospector, Chef Knight or Brotato, and no published cost tables. Nodebuster's radial shape and its purchase juice are not described in any source I could read. The Nodebuster node count is my own count from a removed Steam guide. I found no source on Cookie Clicker's heavenly-upgrade layout (the wiki was paywalled) or the visual design of Hades' Mirror. I found no r/incremental_games threads. Specific tree-juice techniques (travelling light, fog) rest on design inference and one hobby project. The cadence table, the target times and the 100-200-node performance claim are my estimates, which the pacing bot and one profile capture should verify.
